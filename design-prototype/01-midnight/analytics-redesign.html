Analytics, redesigned

The whole Analytics tab again, built around two things you asked for: it knows about your accounts, and the pie chart lets you hide categories, spending or income, to see what is left. Approved so far: no summary card (Home already has it), the Day by day graph at the top, filters reset when the app closes. Sample numbers; the October totals (₹83,899.11 in, ₹88,683.71 out, ₹3,284.58 a day) are the ones on your phone. Not built yet.

The page, and what it looks like once you filter

The two sheets behind the filter chips

What I found on the current page

  • The arc is dead weight. Your October shows "0% of income left" next to −₹4,784.60, so it says nothing on the one month it should matter.
  • One purchase swallows the page. The ₹55,000 laptop makes "Other" about 82% of the pie, turns the daily average into ₹3,284.58 (median ₹130) and sets the whole Day by day scale. That is exactly what hiding a category fixes.
  • It ignores accounts. Every figure is all accounts mixed together, even though My accounts now exists.
  • Two cards say the same thing. "Where it went" and "Where it came from" are separate tall cards, so the page is long before you reach the charts.
  • The summary card and the arc are gone (Home has income, spent and balance). Mean and Median tiles are replaced by the daily average, shown at the top of the Day by day card with last month beside it.
  • Pie and list merge into one Breakdown card with a Spent / Income switch.
  • Day by day moves to the top, keeps its dashed average and clipped spikes, and follows the filters.
  • New: account filter, category filter, By account, and Income vs spending over six months.

How it behaves

  • Account chip. Opens the Accounts sheet. Pick one or several; "All accounts" is the default. The page, the pie, Day by day and Biggest expenses all follow it. Tapping a row in By account is a shortcut for picking just that account.
  • Transfers stay out. Moving money between your own accounts, and paying a card bill, never count as spending or income, whichever account you pick.
  • Categories chip. Opens the Categories sheet with Spent and Income tabs, each a checklist with amounts. Only keeps just that one; All and None are shortcuts.
  • Eye on a row. Hides or shows that category without opening the sheet. A hidden row stays in the list, dimmed and struck through, so it is one tap to bring back.
  • The pie recalculates. Percentages are out of what is still shown, the centre total shrinks, and a "Hidden: Other · ₹52,000 · Show all" line says what is missing.
  • A banner appears whenever any filter is on, naming it, with a Reset. The chips turn white while active.
  • Spent and Income are independent. Hiding Other spending does not touch the Income tab, and the other way round.
  • Weeks and months. Filters stay when you step to another period or switch Month / Week, so you can compare like with like.
  • Empty case. Hide everything and the card says "Nothing left to show" with a Show all button; the donut is not drawn.
  • Tapping a row still opens Transactions for that category and period, now also carrying the chosen accounts.
  • Uncategorized stays its own dashed row with the "Fix N" link, and can be hidden like the rest.

What building it touches

  • Data. Every analytics query takes a list of account ids and a list of hidden category ids, and drops transfers. This is the main work; the screen follows from it.
  • New queries. Totals per account, a six-month income and spending series, and a per-category change against the previous period.
  • State. The two filters live in a small in-memory store next to the period store: kept while the app is open, gone on a fresh start.
  • Spec. UI-UX §6.10 is rewritten (this page replaces the old layout) and implementation §26 gains the filter parameters; both through CRs, then traceability rows and tests.
  • Parts reused. Donut and list code, Day by day, Biggest expenses, the period control, and the account list from My accounts.
  • Left for later. Top payees, recurring payments and the balance trend need data we do not hold reliably yet.

Decided

  1. Hiding a category changes the whole page. The graph and its average, the account rows, the six-month chart and the biggest expenses all follow it, with a banner saying so.
  2. Filters do not survive closing the app. They stay while you switch period or tab.
  3. Page order: controls, Day by day (with the daily average), Breakdown, By account, Income vs spending, Biggest expenses. No summary card.
